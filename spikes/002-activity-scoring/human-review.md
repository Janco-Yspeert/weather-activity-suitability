[human review] Reviewed the Spike 002 evaluator failure. I agree with the core finding that activity sufficiency must not derive its denominator only from the records returned by a partial source: missing timestamps must not make the observed evidence appear more complete than it is.

I do not, however, want to adopt a blanket rule that less than 70% whole-period coverage always forces UNKNOWN. For session/window-based activities, a complete minimum opportunity is itself meaningful positive evidence. A fully observed minimum surf session or sustained ski block may therefore support a conservatively capped positive rating, even when less than 70% of the wider activity period is covered. Broad period coverage remains necessary to make strong claims about the absence of opportunity or the overall quality of the full day.

The accepted calibration should be amended to make that distinction explicit before the implementation is repaired. The evaluator oracle should then be updated to the revised contract, with focused tests covering truncated-but-complete positive opportunities as well as genuinely insufficient evidence.

I also reviewed activity-scoring.ts and consider its current readability below the standard I want for the final submission. The domain logic is difficult to follow because activity semantics, evidence handling, generic observation access, utility scoring and daily aggregation are interleaved in one large module. That cleanup is deliberately deferred until the evaluator-driven behavioural correction is implemented and verified. The next change should be the smallest contract-driven repair needed to establish correct behaviour. Once the revised evaluator passes, a separate behaviour-preserving refactor should improve the scoring module's human readability without changing the accepted scoring outcomes.
